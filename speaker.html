Wiggly electricity, a magnet, and a shove

What happens inside a speaker, hundreds of times every second.

FIG 8.1 · SIGNAL

Wiggly electricity

Your phone sends electricity down the wire that flows one way, then the other, then back again. The faster it flips, the higher the sound.

FIG 8.2 · COIL

The coil becomes a magnet

Electricity flowing round a coil of wire turns it into a magnet. Every time the electricity flips, the coil's north and south swap over.

FIG 8.3 · PUSH

Push, pull, push

The coil sits right next to a big, strong magnet, which pushes it, then pulls it. The coil is glued to the paper cone, so the cone moves in and out too.

FIG 8.4 · AIR

The cone shoves the air

Each push squashes the air into a bunch, and each pull spreads it out. The bunches race to your ear at about 343 metres a second and wiggle your eardrum the same way.

The big idea: a speaker turns wiggles of electricity into wiggles of air. A microphone is the same machine backwards: sound wiggles a coil next to a magnet, and that makes wiggles of electricity.
