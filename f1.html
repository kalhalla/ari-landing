Push the air up, and the car goes down

What happens every time an F1 car goes round a corner.

FIG 6.1 · WINGS

Upside-down wings

An aeroplane wing is shaped to lift. An F1 car's wings, and the shaped floor underneath, are made the other way round, so they push down.

FIG 6.2 · AIR

Air goes up, car goes down

The wings throw the air that hits them upwards. Push air up, and the air pushes the car down. That push is called downforce.

FIG 6.3 · SPEED

Faster means much more

Go twice as fast and you get four times the downforce. That's why F1 cars grip best at high speed.

FIG 6.4 · GRIP

Pressed into the road

The harder the tyres are pushed into the road, the more they grip. More grip means faster corners. The cost: wings also slow the car down on the straights.

The big idea: an F1 car's wings are an aeroplane wing turned upside down. At high speed they push down harder than the car weighs, so in theory an F1 car could drive upside down on a ceiling, at around 200 km/h. Nobody has actually tried it.
