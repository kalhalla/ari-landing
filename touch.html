Wires, charge, a dip, and some maths

What your phone does every time you touch it.

FIG 5.1 · GRID

Invisible wires

Under the glass are rows and columns of wires so thin and clear you can't see them. Where a row crosses a column, it makes a crossing point.

FIG 5.2 · CHARGE

A tiny charge

The phone pushes a tiny charge into each row. Where it crosses a column, an invisible electric field loops up through the glass and back down.

FIG 5.3 · SOAK

Your finger soaks it up

Your body conducts electricity. Touch the glass and your finger pulls away some of the field, so the crossings under it hold less charge.

FIG 5.4 · FIND

The phone does the maths

Over a hundred times a second, the phone checks every crossing, finds where the charge dipped, and works out the middle of the dip, even between two wires. It can find several dips at once.

The big idea: your finger becomes part of the circuit. Anything that conducts electricity like a finger, even a sausage, can work the screen. Things that don't, like a woolly glove or a wooden pencil, can't.
