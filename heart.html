Fig. 09 · The heart

How does your heart pump blood?

It's two pumps side by side, with doors that only open one way. Tap the heart to make it beat, follow a blood cell all the way round, then break a valve.

Fill, squeeze, snap, repeat

What your heart does, roughly once every second, your whole life.

01 · FILL

The top rooms fill

Blood coming back from your body and your lungs pours into the two top rooms, called the atria.

02 · SQUEEZE

The pumps squeeze

The bottom rooms, the ventricles, have thick walls of muscle. They squeeze hard and push the blood out.

03 · SNAP

Valves snap shut

One-way valves slam shut so blood can't go backwards. Lub is the first pair closing, dub is the second. That's the sound a doctor hears.

04 · TWO LOOPS

Lungs, then body

The right side sends blood to your lungs to pick up oxygen. The left side sends it all round your body.

The big idea: your heart is two pumps side by side, and the valves make sure blood only ever goes one way. It's about the size of your fist, and it beats about 100,000 times a day.
