What's happening inside the panel, step by step.

01 · LIGHT

Sunlight lands

Light arrives in tiny packets of energy called photons. Point the panel straight at the sun and the most packets land on it.

02 · SILICON

Electrons shake loose

A photon can knock an electron free inside the silicon, like one snooker ball knocking another. Tap the zoomed-in cell to try it.

03 · JUNCTION

A one-way push

The cell is made of two layers of silicon. Where they meet, the electrons get pushed in one direction only, towards the top.

04 · CIRCUIT

Out through the wire

The electrons flow out through a wire, through your kettle or lights, and back into the bottom of the cell. That flow is electricity.

The big idea: a solar cell doesn't store anything. It only makes electricity while light is landing on it, which is why houses with solar panels use batteries, and why it's smart to run the washing machine at lunchtime.
