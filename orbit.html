Why the space station stays up there.

FIG 7.1 · PULL

Gravity pulls

Earth pulls everything towards its middle. Even up where the space station flies, gravity is still about 90% as strong as on the ground.

FIG 7.2 · SIDEWAYS

Go sideways, fast

Throw a ball sideways and it falls in a curve. The faster you throw it, the further it goes before it lands.

FIG 7.3 · CURVE

The ground curves away

Earth is round. Go fast enough and the ground curves away underneath you just as fast as you fall towards it.

FIG 7.4 · ORBIT

It never lands

At about 7.7 km per second, it falls and misses, again and again. The space station goes all the way round Earth about every 90 minutes.

The big idea: a satellite is falling all the time. It just moves sideways so fast that it keeps missing the ground. Astronauts float because they're falling too, right along with their space station.
